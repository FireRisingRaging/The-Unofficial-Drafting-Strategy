      Before You Begin

      How This Book Works

      Click the right-hand page to turn forward, and the left-hand page to turn back. The pages curl in three dimensions as they turn — try it now.

      Your scroll wheel and the arrow keys work too, and on a touchscreen you can tap either side of the spread. The ☰ button in the corner opens a menu that jumps straight to any page.

      psst — the table of contents on the page before this one is clickable too.

      Chapter One

      Adding a New Page

      Open index.html and find the #content-source block near the bottom. Every page is one <section class="page"> — copy one, paste it where you'd like the new page to appear, and edit the text inside.

      A minimal page looks like this

      <section class="page" id="my-page" data-title="My Page Title">
        <div class="page-body">
          <h1 class="page-title">My Page Title</h1>
          <p>Your text here.</p>
        </div>
      </section>

      The page number and its entry in the Table of Contents both appear automatically — you never type a number.

      Chapter One, Continued

      Adding Images

      Replace this with your own file, e.g. <img src="images/your-photo.jpg">

      Keep image files in an images/ folder next to index.html and reference them with a relative path. Images automatically scale to fit the page width.

      Chapter Two

      Organizing Sections

      Pages appear in the order you write them — to reorder the book, just move a <section> block up or down in the file.

      Give related pages the same data-section label (shown in small print at the foot of each page) to group them into chapters, the way "Chapter One" appears on both pages 3 and 4 of this book.

      To leave a page out of the Contents list entirely — useful for a title page or a colophon — add data-no-toc to its <section> tag instead of data-title.

      Chapter Two, Continued

      Quick Reference

      • .page-eyebrow — small label above a title
      • .page-title — the big heading
      • .handwritten — a scrawled note
      • .callout — a highlighted box
      • .caption — small text under an image
      • .divider — a thin horizontal rule

      Everything else — paragraphs, headings, ordered and unordered lists — is styled automatically.
